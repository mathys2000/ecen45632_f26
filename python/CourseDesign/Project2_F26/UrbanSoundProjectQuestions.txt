Typical Benchmark Curves

Depending on whether you train from scratch or use transfer learning (e.g., pre-training on ESC-50 or AudioSet), healthy convergence usually follows these patterns: [1] (https://arxiv.org/html/2012.10056v1), [2] (https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09321v1)
• From-Scratch 2D CNNs: Using log-Mel spectrograms or MFCCs, models typically stabilize between 15 and 25 epochs. Validation accuracy commonly lands between 73% and 86%, with validation loss settling around 0.25 to 0.50. [1] (https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3441417.3441431), [2] (https://ijsrem.com/download/urban-sound-classification/fulltext.pdf), [3] (https://medium.com/@beytullahyayla1/classifying-urban-sounds-using-2d-cnns-and-mel-spectrograms-f1aa049218c2), [4] (https://noiselab.ucsd.edu/ECE228_2019/Reports/Report36.pdf)
• Transfer Learning / Multi-Feature CNNs: Fine-tuning pre-trained models (such as YAMNet) or stacking multiple features (MFCC + Mel Spectrogram + Chroma) pushes validation accuracy higher, often reaching 90% to 94%. [1] (https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09321v1), [2] (https://medium.com/@beytullahyayla1/classifying-urban-sounds-using-2d-cnns-and-mel-spectrograms-f1aa049218c2), [3] (https://arxiv.org/html/2012.10056v1)

Diagnosing Your Loss & Accuracy Curves


Curve Pattern	Diagnosis	Common Audio-Specific Causes & Fixes
Train loss \(\downarrow \) smoothly, Val loss \(\downarrow \) then \(\uparrow \) (Train acc ≈ 99%, Val acc plateaus at 70%)	Overfitting	Data leakage or memorization. Ensure you use UrbanSound8K’s predefined 10 folds rather than a random split, as random splits put clips from the same source recording in both train and validation sets. Add SpecAugment (time/frequency masking), dropout, or weight decay.
Val loss is lower (or Val acc is higher) than Train early on	Regularization artifact or easy split	Dropout / BatchNorm behavior. Dropout is active during training (hurting train metrics) and turned off during validation. If this persists across all epochs, check if one validation fold contains mostly distinct, easy classes (like car horn vs. engine idling).
Both Train & Val loss stay high and flat	Underfitting	Learning rate or feature scaling issues. Normalize your Mel spectrograms or MFCCs (zero mean, unit variance) before feeding them into the CNN. Try lowering the Adam learning rate (e.g., 1e-3 to 3e-4) or adding convolutional capacity.
Val loss spikes wildly every few epochs	Unstable gradients / Batch variance	Small batch size or unpadded lengths. UrbanSound8K clips vary in length (≤ 4s). Pad or crop all waveforms to a fixed duration before computing spectrograms, use a batch size of 32–64, and add a learning rate scheduler (ReduceLROnPlateau).


Practical PyTorch Tracking Snippet

To plot and inspect these curves cleanly during training, track epoch-level averages rather than single-batch metrics:
python
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

def plot_training_curves(history):
    epochs = range(1, len(history["train_loss"]) + 1)
    fig, (ax1, ax2) = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(12, 4))

    ax1.plot(epochs, history["train_loss"], label="Train Loss")
    ax1.plot(epochs, history["val_loss"], label="Val Loss")
    ax1.set_title("Cross-Entropy Loss")
    ax1.set_xlabel("Epoch")
    ax1.legend()

    ax2.plot(epochs, history["train_acc"], label="Train Accuracy")
    ax2.plot(epochs, history["val_acc"], label="Val Accuracy")
    ax2.set_title("Classification Accuracy")
    ax2.set_xlabel("Epoch")
    ax2.legend()

    plt.tight_layout()
    plt.show()
